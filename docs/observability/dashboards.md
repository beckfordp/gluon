# Grafana dashboards guide

How the gluon dashboards are organised, what each one is for and how to read it.
The decision behind the layout is [ADR 0027](../adr/0027-dashboard-architecture.md).
Every dashboard links here from its **Guide** header link and has a collapsed
**About this dashboard** row with the short version.

Open Grafana with `bin/obs up`, then go to http://localhost:3000 (the home page is the
Christmas Rush overview).

## How they fit together

Three levels, one folder each. Start at the top and drill down:

| Folder | Dashboard | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1-Overview | [Christmas Rush](#christmas-rush) | Is the order journey healthy right now? |
| 2-Services | [Service](#service) | Is *this* service healthy, and if not, why? |
| 2-Services | [Checkout & payments](#checkout--payments) | Where in browse → checkout → payment are we losing orders? |
| 2-Services | [Dispatch & async flow](#dispatch--async-flow) | Is the backlog after payment in the warehouse or in the pipeline? |
| 3-Platform | [Pods & runtime](#pods--runtime) | Are the pods and JVMs out of CPU or memory? |
| 3-Platform | [Data stores](#data-stores) | Are Postgres or Redis the bottleneck? |
| 3-Platform | [Kafka & Debezium](#kafka--debezium) | Is the event pipeline healthy? |

Conventions used everywhere:
- **Symptoms first, causes underneath.** Higher levels show what customers feel: rate, errors
  and duration (RED). The platform level shows utilisation, saturation and errors of machines
  (USE). Alerts (PERF-11) fire on symptoms.
- **A dashed red line is an SLO**: checkout p95 1.5s, 5xx 1%, event age 2 min. Pod
  CPU/memory lines (80/90%) are capacity warnings, not SLOs.
- **No data is normal** for failure panels (DLQ, breakers, 5xx) until that failure happens.
  The scrape-targets panel on *Pods & runtime* tells you whether the silence is real.
- **Navigation.** The *gluon dashboards* header dropdown keeps the time range and variables.
  Panels with a ↗ icon link to the next level down. Clicking a series that has a service or
  pod opens that service or pod.
- **Dashboards are code.** The JSON is generated by `bin/obs-dashboards` from
  `infra/k8s/observability/dashboards/`. Edits made in the Grafana UI are not saved.

## Christmas Rush

**Purpose.** One screen for load tests and the Christmas Rush demo. It shows demand,
checkout outcomes and latency against the SLO, errors by service, dispatch against
arrivals, event age, money held and saturation. It has 12 panels, so it fits one screen.

**How to read it.**
1. *Demand & checkout.* Orders/s is the load. Checkout success % is the headline: declines and
   stock-outs pull it down legitimately, so read it together with the outcome mix. Latency
   should stay under the 1.5s line.
2. *Errors (symptoms).* 5xx % by service is the fault signal (SLO 1%). 4xx % is mostly business
   rejections: payment declined (402), out of stock (409), validation (400). Click a service's
   line to open it on **Service**.
3. *Flow & capacity.* If dispatch stays below arrivals, the people backlog grows. Event age over
   2 min means the pipeline is behind. If the money-held count keeps climbing, captures are
   stuck. CPU/memory % of limit are causes: check them when latency rises. Click a pod to open
   it.

**Drill down:** the row panels link to *Checkout & payments* and *Dispatch & async flow*.

## Service

**Purpose.** One templated dashboard for all eight services. Choose one in the **Service**
dropdown. It replaces the old `gluon-red` dashboard.

**How to read it** (top to bottom = symptom to cause):
1. *Golden signals*: request rate, error % (4xx/5xx against 1%), errors by route and
   status, latency p50/p95/p99.
2. *Dependencies & resilience*: outbound latency and errors per target, retries and
   breaker state. A slow or failing dependency shows up here before it shows in the golden
   signals.
3. *Kafka consumers*: event age against 2 min, lag by topic, DLQ publishes and stream restarts.
4. *Database & runtime*: query p95 and errors, pool in use/pending and wait p95 (queueing for
   a connection), JVM heap, cats-effect CPU starvation and GC time.

## Checkout & payments

**Purpose.** Follows the money: basket and stock → checkout → authorize → capture. It
shows business outcomes at each step, not just HTTP codes.

**How to read it.**
1. *Checkout*: success %, outcome mix, latency against SLO, and order-service's downstream latency
   per hop (click a hop to open that service).
2. *Payments*: authorize and capture outcomes, voids and expired authorizations, and money held
   (uncaptured count and oldest age). Declines are expected. Capture failures and a rising
   oldest age are not.
3. *Basket & stock*: reservation outcomes (insufficient = out of stock), cart operations,
   catalog cache hit %, registrations and logins.

## Dispatch & async flow

**Purpose.** After payment there are two kinds of backlog. The **people backlog** is
fulfilment tasks waiting for warehouse staff. The **system backlog** is events waiting in
outbox → Debezium → Kafka → consumer. This dashboard puts them side by side so you can tell
which one is growing.

**How to read it.**
1. *People backlog*: dispatch against arrival, backlog and oldest task age by state, step
   duration p95 (the slow step is the bottleneck), order time-in-state p95.
2. *System backlog*: event age against 2 min, consumer-group lag, Debezium ms behind source
   (the outbox hop), order status transitions, DLQ and restarts, notification email outcomes.

## Pods & runtime

**Purpose.** Machine health (USE) for the service pods and their JVMs. Choose pods in the
**Pod** dropdown.

**How to read it.**
1. *Pods*: CPU and memory against limits, throttling %, memory % of limit (90% line), restarts
   and last-termination OOMKilled. Throttling and high memory come before latency rises and
   OOMKills (see TD-27).
2. *JVM & cats-effect*: heap, GC time, CPU starvation, threads, DB-pool pending and wait for every
   service. Click a series to open that service.
3. *Monitoring*: every scrape target should be green.

## Data stores

**Purpose.** The per-service Postgres databases and the Redis caches, seen from the
server side (exporters) and the client side (service query metrics). It replaces the old
`gluon-infra` dashboard; Kafka Connect moved to *Kafka & Debezium*.

**How to read it.** Postgres rollbacks, locks and any deadlock are errors. Client query p95
rising while transactions stay flat points to contention. For Redis, a falling hit ratio
sends load back to Postgres.

## Kafka & Debezium

**Purpose.** The event pipeline every journey depends on. Filter by service in the
**Service** dropdown (default: all).

**How to read it.**
1. *Producers & consumers (purekafka)*: lag, DLQ, restarts, retries, breaker transitions and
   state.
2. *Broker*: consumer-group lag and topic write rate. Lag rising while the write rate is flat
   means consumers can't keep up.
3. *Kafka Connect & Debezium*: connectors and tasks should be RUNNING and Debezium connected.
   Behind source is the outbox delay.

## Changing a dashboard

1. Edit the Python in `infra/k8s/observability/dashboards/` (`overview.py`, `services.py`,
   `platform_dashboards.py`; shared look and navigation in `lib.py`).
2. `bin/obs-dashboards` regenerates the JSON. `bin/obs-dashboards --test` runs the checks:
   panel budget (12 for the overview, 20 elsewhere), links resolve, every dashboard has a
   description, an About row and a Guide link, and the committed JSON is current.
3. `bin/obs up` re-applies the ConfigMaps. Grafana reloads within 30s.
4. With traffic flowing (`bin/run-acceptance`), `tests/performance/scripts/check-obs-dashboards.sh`
   checks every query live.
